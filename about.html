I'm Yile Liu, an undergraduate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the ZJU-UIUC Institute, currently in Haining, China. I'll be on the Urbana-Champaign campus from Fall 2027, and I expect to graduate in 2029.

My interests are compilers and low-level systems — I like problems where the output has to actually run, and where "it works" is something you can measure.

Work

LLVMIRToLC3Pass — an out-of-tree LLVM pass, written in C++, that lowers a subset of LLVM IR generated from C down to LC-3 assembly: a 16-bit ISA with eight registers.

LC-3 has no multiply and no divide instruction, so mul, udiv and urem are synthesized from addition and shifts. The calling convention started out passing arguments in registers; when that ran out of registers I rewrote it as a stack frame matching the LC-3 C runtime protocol used in the course.

The deliberate simplification is that every virtual register gets its own stack slot — a load and a store around every operation, and no register allocation at all. That is where the next version starts, not something I overlooked.
