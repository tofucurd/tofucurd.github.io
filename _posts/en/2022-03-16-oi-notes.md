## Line endings

Windows: `\r\n` (ASCII 13, 10)

macOS / Unix / Linux: `\n` (ASCII 10)

Which one you get is decided by where the file was created, not by where it runs.

## EOF

EOF is -1.

## Reading a character

`scanf("%c",&ch)` is equivalent to `ch=getchar()`.

## cstring

`strlen` is $O(n)$.

## Floyd

Running the loop order `i,j,k` three times is fine.

## if-else

```cpp
if(expr_a)if(expr_b)expr_c
else expr_d
```

Here `expr_d` binds to `expr_a`.

## Enlarging the stack

Windows: `"-Wl,--stack=<size>"`

Linux: `"-Wl,-z,stack-size=<size>"`

macOS: `"-Wl,-stack_size,<size>"`

`-Wl` means the argument that follows is handed to the linker. Linkers differ across platforms, hence three different flags. Also, `ulimit -s <size>` does nothing on macOS.

## Sanitizers

What they do: catch runtime errors.

How to use: `"-fsanitize=address,undefined,leak"`

On Linux, g++ supports AddressSanitizer, UBSanitizer and LeakSanitizer.

On macOS, Apple clang supports AddressSanitizer and UBSanitizer; Homebrew g++ supports neither.

On Windows, clang supports AddressSanitizer, UBSanitizer and LeakSanitizer; MinGW supports none of them.
